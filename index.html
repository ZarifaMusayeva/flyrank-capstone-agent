CTR & Engagement Opportunity Scoring in Search Intelligence

A Random Forest Predictive Framework for Content Optimization

By Zarifa Musayeva | FlyRank ML Track Capstone

1. Abstract

This study addresses the problem of identifying underperforming web pages that capture fewer search clicks than expected given their impressions and ranking position. Using the FlyRank Search Intelligence dataset, we engineered features and trained a Random Forest Regressor to predict baseline click performance. Our model achieved an R² score of 0.5638, significantly outperforming the naive baseline MSE (259.72 vs 595.51). By calculating the residual gap between expected and actual performance, we built an automated Opportunity Score engine. This provides actionable content optimization recommendations to maximize organic visibility and CTR.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

Digital marketing and SEO teams often struggle to prioritize which content assets require optimization. While high impression counts indicate good query visibility, low click-through rates (CTR) highlight a failure to capture search intent. This research provides a decision-support system that quantifies underperformance, allowing content teams to focus resources on pages with the highest unrealized click potential.

3. Data Source & Preparation

The analysis was conducted on the FlyRank/internship-warehouse release using the fact_content_daily_performance table.

  • Sample Size: 50,000 daily performance records.
  • Filters: Excluded entries with impressions ≤ 100 and missing position metrics to eliminate noise.
  • Features Utilized: gsc_sum_position, gsc_impressions.
  • Target Label: gsc_clicks.

4. Methodology

We framed the task as a non-linear regression problem. The dataset was split into an 80/20 train-test ratio. We selected a Random Forest Regressor (100 estimators) to capture non-linear relationships between search ranking position and click generation.

Opportunity Score Calculation:

Opportunity Score = Predicted Clicks - Actual Clicks

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

The model relies primarily on positional and impression metrics without incorporating qualitative content factors such as title tag sentiment or search intent seasonality. Results should be interpreted as directional decision support rather than exact predictive guarantees.
